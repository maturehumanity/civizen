# Funding and Sustainability Plan

**Purpose:** Practical strategic reference for how Civizen approaches funding and long-term sustainability while the project remains under active development.

**Status:** Draft guidance. This document does not select a financing model, authorize capital acceptance, or replace counsel-approved legal instruments.

**Controlling public policy:** [`funding-and-financial-integrity.md`](../../02-policies/institutional/funding-and-financial-integrity.md) · [`investor-interest-non-offering-notice.md`](../../02-policies/institutional/investor-interest-non-offering-notice.md)

Related strategy documents: [`funding-options.md`](./funding-options.md) · [`funding-readiness-roadmap.md`](./funding-readiness-roadmap.md)

---

## 1. Current position

Civizen is currently founder-led. The platform and public project are under active development.

A definitive long-term funding model has **not** been selected. Potential routes include grants and philanthropy, institutional or government funding, private investment, commercial revenue, community support, or a combination. No single route is assumed.

Funding decisions should follow demonstrated needs, credible opportunities, institutional readiness, and mission protections—not premature commitment to a financing architecture.

Public funding surfaces collect inquiries and expressions of interest only. They do not process investment payments, donations, grants, or other capital.

---

## 2. What funding is needed for

At this stage, funding needs are described as categories rather than detailed budgets. Detailed budgets should be prepared when a concrete opportunity or pilot requires them.

| Category | Examples of use |
| --- | --- |
| Engineering and product development | Platform features, quality, release continuity |
| Infrastructure and AI | Hosting, tooling, model/API costs, reliability |
| Security and privacy | Hardening, audits, incident readiness, data protection |
| Legal and institutional setup | Entity structure, counsel, compliance foundations |
| Research and education | Study materials, research partnerships, curriculum work |
| Contributor compensation | Paid contributors, contractors, fellows where authorized |
| Pilots and partnerships | Scoped deployments with communities or institutions |
| Community development and country activation | Local readiness, facilitation, onboarding support |
| Communications and adoption | Public identity, outreach, documentation |
| Ongoing operations | Administration, accounting, reserves, day-to-day continuity |

---

## 3. Funding principles

Regardless of source, Civizen funding should observe these principles:

1. **Mission first** — funding must serve the mission rather than control it.
2. **Diversification** — avoid dependence on a single government, corporation, investor, donor, or institution.
3. **Governance separation** — financial support does not automatically confer governance authority.
4. **Transparency and accountability** — material funding relationships should be identifiable, classified, and reportable as policy requires.
5. **Contributor compensation** — people who do substantive work may be compensated under lawful budgets and agreements; participation alone does not create a claim on proceeds.
6. **Institutional independence** — preserve long-term capacity to act in the public interest.
7. **Responsible reserves** — maintain prudent reserves when funds are available.
8. **No pay-to-participate civic core** — core civic participation must not become a paid privilege.
9. **Public-interest and open-source orientation** — preserve Civizen’s public-interest character and open-source orientation.

These principles align with the public integrity statement in [`funding-and-financial-integrity.md`](../../02-policies/institutional/funding-and-financial-integrity.md).

---

## 4. Possible funding model

Civizen is **not currently committing** to a single financing model.

Potential sources may include, alone or in combination:

- grants;
- philanthropy;
- international institutions;
- universities and research institutions;
- governments and public-interest programs;
- corporate sponsorship;
- community donations;
- mission-aligned investment;
- commercial / earned revenue;
- future financial mechanisms, if legally and institutionally appropriate.

A diversified model may ultimately be preferable because it reduces capture risk and single-source fragility. Comparison of routes: [`funding-options.md`](./funding-options.md).

Token, crypto, or other monetary mechanisms remain provisional unless separately adopted under counsel-approved governance. See also the conditional materials indexed in this folder’s [README](./README.md).

---

## 5. Funding progression

Progression is stage-based, not speculative funding rounds.

| Stage | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Current** | Founder-led development and validation |
| **Next** | Public credibility, product maturity, community building, and pilot readiness |
| **Then** | Pilot partnerships and targeted external funding |
| **Later** | Scaling and diversified sustainable financing |
| **Long term** | Financially and institutionally sustainable Civizen ecosystem |

Readiness indicators and practical steps: [`funding-readiness-roadmap.md`](./funding-readiness-roadmap.md).

---

## 6. Sustainability

Over time, Civizen should reduce dependence on fundraising alone.

Potential earned-revenue mechanisms may be considered later if mission-compatible and properly authorized. Examples at a high level only (not approved business models):

- institutional services;
- implementation and support;
- optional professional tools;
- training and education;
- integrations / APIs;
- research partnerships;
- marketplace or ecosystem services where appropriate.

Any commercial path must keep core civic participation free of paywalls and must not surrender mission control. Classification of earned revenue relative to other funding classes follows [`funding-and-financial-integrity.md`](../../02-policies/institutional/funding-and-financial-integrity.md).

---

## 7. Relationship between awareness, adoption, and funding

Civizen should not begin with indiscriminate fundraising outreach.

It should progressively establish:

- a credible public identity;
- clear philosophy and purpose;
- functioning software;
- open-source credibility;
- public communication;
- community participation;
- pilot opportunities;
- academic and institutional relationships;
- evidence of adoption and impact.

These foundations make later funding conversations substantially stronger and more selective. Awareness and adoption are enabling conditions for funding—not substitutes for institutional readiness or legal compliance.

---

## 8. Mission protection

Civizen should actively avoid:

| Risk | Concern |
| --- | --- |
| Political capture | Funding that subordinates the mission to partisan or state agendas |
| Commercial capture | Sponsors or revenue terms that distort product, research, or governance |
| Investor capture | Financing that converts mission assets or civic authority into private control |
| Donor dependency | Single-donor leverage over priorities or speech |
| Founder dependency | Structures that cannot continue beyond founder-led stewardship |
| Single-source dependence | Fragility and leverage from any one government, firm, investor, donor, or institution |

Financial support must not require concealment of conflicts, misuse of data, diversion of restricted funds, or surrender of the mission. See Independence provisions in [`funding-and-financial-integrity.md`](../../02-policies/institutional/funding-and-financial-integrity.md) and [`conflict-of-interest-policy-v0.1.md`](./conflict-of-interest-policy-v0.1.md).

---

## 9. Immediate priorities

Practical sequence for the near term:

1. Continue improving the working Civizen platform.
2. Strengthen public documentation and messaging.
3. Build awareness through Civizen and founder channels.
4. Grow open-source and community credibility.
5. Identify useful pilot opportunities.
6. Clarify the legal and institutional structure needed to accept funding.
7. Identify funding opportunities that naturally fit those pilots.
8. Pursue targeted conversations rather than broad fundraising.
9. Develop detailed budgets when an actual funding opportunity requires them.
10. Expand this funding framework as real funding routes emerge.

Do not create separate grant, investor, government, or commercial strategy documents until Civizen is actively pursuing those routes and the extra detail is useful.
