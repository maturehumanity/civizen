# Community Readiness and Program Availability Framework

## Notice

Civizen readiness measures participation and operational capacity within the platform. It is not an election, referendum, governmental recognition, territorial claim, jurisdictional mandate, or transfer of legal authority.

## 1. Purpose

This framework describes how Civizen may assess **participation readiness** and **operational capacity** before expanding or unlocking certain platform programs, features, or community processes for a geographic community, language community, or other published program scope.

At the current stage, readiness is a Civizen platform and program concept. It does not by itself establish public authority, but it may help measure whether the network has developed sufficient participation, integrity, representation, and operational capacity for later public and institutional consideration.

## Relationship to the Long-Term Mission

Civizen's long-term goal includes a legitimate pathway toward recognized planetary citizenship. Readiness data may inform that journey, but population percentages, account counts, or platform declarations cannot substitute for democratic consent, representative legitimacy, legal authorization, constitutional safeguards, or institutional recognition.

## 2. What Readiness Is Not

Readiness does **not**:

- create or imply governmental recognition
- alter borders, sovereignty, or nationality
- constitute an election, referendum, or public mandate
- bring any legal constitution “into force”
- transfer legal authority outside Civizen
- replace national, local, cultural, or religious institutions

Country or world “activation” language in older materials, if any remain, should be read only as program readiness or network availability within Civizen.

## 3. Readiness Principle

Civizen may recognize a readiness stage when published participation and operational criteria for a defined program scope are met or approached, subject to integrity, safety, and capacity review.

Criteria may include verified participation levels, conduct and safety posture, operational staffing or steward capacity, technical integrity, and documentation completeness. Thresholds are operational planning tools, not demographic claims of public consent or majority governance of a nation or the world.

## 4. Stages (illustrative)

- **Exploratory** — limited pilots; no broad program unlock
- **Building readiness** — growing participation and operational capacity; review in progress
- **Program available** — published features or community processes available within Civizen for that scope
- **Paused or constrained** — availability reduced for integrity, safety, capacity, or legal compliance reasons

Exact stage names and unlocks may be refined in product and stewardship procedures.

## 5. Effects Inside Civizen

When a readiness stage unlocks a program, effects are limited to Civizen platform status, features, and community processes expressly described for that unlock (for example eligibility for certain roles, badges, or workflows).

Current readiness effects do not by themselves create legal citizenship, public-law voting rights, diplomatic status, or governmental power.

## 6. Review

Before recognizing a material readiness change, Civizen should apply proportionate review: participation and integrity checks, operational capacity review, technical integrity where relevant, and an auditable record of the decision within Civizen’s governance and stewardship systems.

## 7. Relationship to Civic Status

Separate policies describe member, verified, citizen, active, and governance-eligible statuses inside Civizen. Where “active” or program eligibility depends on readiness for a scope, that dependence is platform-internal only and must be read together with the World Citizenship and Civic Status Notice.
