## 1. Program purpose and duration

**Duration:** **18–24 months** (planning mid: **21 months**).  
**Objective:** Convert civilization-scale frameworks into a professionally reviewable program that can receive initial funding and decide, with evidence, whether (and how) major implementation may begin.

**Independently valuable even if later implementation funding is delayed:** published constitutional drafts and anti-capture analyses; reviewed inventory with dissent preserved; architecture/threat-model reports; domain study volumes; standards sketches and test-suite seeds; demonstrator artifacts that never held authoritative data; organizational charters and open financial reporting for this phase.

## 4. System-inventory validation method

**Scope:** Every current inventory row (today **467** in `12`; count may change). Treat the catalog as a living hypothesis.

### 4.1 Per-entry checklist

For each system ID, reviewers record structured findings on:

1. Necessity (keep / merge / split / defer / reject)  
2. Missing systems discovered  
3. Duplication with other IDs  
4. Inappropriate centralization risk  
5. Institutional authority (who may be authoritative)  
6. Legal feasibility (sampled traditions)  
7. Affected rights  
8. Safety and abuse risk  
9. Correct build / adopt / adapt / federate / regulate / integrate decision  
10. Dependencies (and cycles)  
11. Professional and community representation gaps  
12. Maturity and cost assumptions (credible / weak / absent)

**Uncertainty fields (mandatory):** `confidence` (high/medium/low); `dissent_summary`; `open_questions`; `reviewer_panel_ids`.

The published catalog **must preserve minority reports**. Consensus language is allowed only where panels explicitly record agreement.

### 4.2 Special tracks

| Track | Objects | Extra requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operate-directly | ~44 proposed Civizen-operated systems | Necessity test; capture risk; exit plan; why not standards-only |
| Never-centralize | ~42 flagged systems | Confirm prohibition; alternative federated/J patterns; red-team “convenience centralization” |

### 4.3 Cadence

- Months 2–4: method pilot on 40 diverse entries  
- Months 4–16: rolling waves by domain  
- Months 16–19: reconciliation, gap list, versioned catalog v0.2  
- Continuous: public changelog of contested items

## 5. Financial-model validation method

Independent specialists (panels + contracted cost engineers) review `11`/`13` hypotheses:

| Topic | Review focus | Typical evidence upgrade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quantities & unit costs | Challenge spreadsheet drivers | RFIs, quotations, market studies |
| Workforce | Role mix, regions, productivity | Benchmarks, labor market studies |
| Jurisdiction archetypes | Replace single averages | Government costing input (sampled) |
| Operator requirements | Topology, independence, OpEx | Operator prototypes (design-validation only) |
| Institutional integrations | Envelope realism | Institution SOW samples |
| Contingency & reserves | Adequacy vs risk register | Actuarial / insurance assessment |
| Inflation & FX | Explicit assumptions | Scenario tables with rate dates |
| Annual cash flow | Maintain vs expand vs replace | Recast after inventory validation |
| Duplication / omissions | Shared-core vs domain | Inventory crosswalk |
| Core vs ecosystem | Responsibility map | Funding-party stress tests |
| 5/10/20-year scenarios | Plausibility, not destiny | Sensitivity + kill criteria |

**Output:** `Cost Model Validation Report v0.1` with revised bands **or** explicit “insufficient evidence — do not capitalize at claimed precision.”

## 6. Safe demonstrators

### 6.1 Allowed

Demonstrators that:

- use **synthetic** or **explicitly authorized test** data only;  
- have **no legally authoritative effect**;  
- avoid **real-money custody**;  
- avoid **binding elections or governance decisions**;  
- avoid **production identity claims** about real populations;  
- are **clearly labeled** as non-production;  
- are **safely stoppable** within documented time/cost;  
- test architecture and usability hypotheses;  
- produce reusable **standards, test suites, or open components**.

Illustrative candidates: federation directory sandbox; consent-ledger demo on synthetic personas; case-pattern UX lab; conformance test harnesses; offline-sync kits; accessibility reference front-ends; operator-certification exam prototype (no live production ops).

### 6.2 Prohibited before major-build readiness gates

- Authoritative production cutovers  
- Binding civil registration or population-scale identity issuance  
- Custody of real payment floats or treasury assets  
- Binding votes, elections, or coercive administrative decisions  
- Centralized stores for never-centralize classes (police, borders, military C2, etc.)  
- Presenting demos as “Civizen production” or “recognized citizenship”  
- Irreversible migrations of institutional systems of record  

Failure mode: **revision, narrower scope, or safe pause** — never relabeling a demonstrator as production.

## 7. Institutional formation (initial ecosystem)

No single founder, funder, government, company, or technical operator may hold unilateral authority over the program.

| Body | Role | Independence notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civizen core steward | Coordinates delivery of validation workstreams; cannot alone approve production gates | Fiduciary duties; public reporting |
| Independent governance & rights body | Charters, anti-capture, rights reviews | Ring-fenced funding (see WS-16 share + dedicated seats) |
| Independent security & privacy assurance | Threat models, assurance letters | Cannot be hired solely by delivery leads for gate sign-off |
| Scientific & technical advisory structures | Methods, architecture alternatives | Minority reports preserved |
| Domain councils | Advise on domain studies; not regulators | Conflict rules; community seats |
| Financial & procurement controls | Multi-entity controls; audit | Dual control on reserve draws |
| Public-interest transparency office | Publications, disclosure calendar | Cannot suppress panel dissent |
| Whistleblower & complaint mechanisms | External channel | Protection policy before M6 |
| Jurisdictional / international participants | Consultation seats | No veto sold for funding |
| Temporary arrangements | Time-boxed founding instruments | Explicit sunset → mature governance |

Transition plan required by Month 12 draft / Month 20 ratification pathway (even if ratification occurs later).

Detail for panels and studies: **document `15`**.

## 8. Readiness gates (major implementation may begin only if)

These gates refine `09` G1–G11 and `11` RG1–RG10 for the **exit from validation**.

| Gate | Measurable evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **V-G1** Constitutional legitimacy pathway | Published draft instruments + independent governance review + public comment summary |
| **V-G2** Rights & anti-capture | Independent rights report; capture scenarios tested; complaint channel live |
| **V-G3** Independently reviewed architecture | Alternative analysis + threat model accepted or residual risks recorded |
| **V-G4** Credible security & privacy model | Assurance findings remediated or accepted; crypto agility plan |
| **V-G5** Validated operator topology | Design-validation operators meet diversity/independence criteria |
| **V-G6** Validated capability & system inventory | Catalog v0.2 with dissent preserved; operate/never-centralize tracks closed |
| **V-G7** Priority domain feasibility | All ten priority studies delivered or gated deferrals documented |
| **V-G8** Institutional participation | Defined count of **participating** J/institutions (per `11` definitions) with named owners |
| **V-G9** Workforce & procurement plan | Hiring/procurement capacity matched to proposed Year-1 burn |
| **V-G10** Validated five-year financial model | Independent cost validation report; revised bands published |
