# Civizen Institutional Blueprint

**Status: Working Institutional Blueprint**

**Working Draft 0.1**  
**Institutional Architecture for the Civizen System**

This document is a **working institutional blueprint** and an authoritative project reference. It describes a proposed long-term architecture. It does **not** assert that the named legal entities, boards, councils, constitutions, or funding instruments already legally exist, have been formed, or have been adopted as binding public policy.

Proposed structures in this Blueprint — including a Civizen Foundation, a commercial public-benefit company, fiduciary boards, councils, reserved-matter mechanisms, a future Funding Constitution, and a future Contributor Framework — remain **proposed** until they are actually established through valid legal formation, governing documents, and applicable law.

Civizen is not owned by investors and is not controlled by any government. Legal entities that may later hold assets or operate programs would do so as instruments of Civizen's mission, not as conventional private owners of the civic system.

---

## 1. Purpose of This Blueprint

Civizen is intended to transform the principles of *Mature Humanity* from a philosophical vision into a practical, participatory system that can operate across countries, institutions, communities, and eventually the world.

*Mature Humanity* is a foundational intellectual source and parent vision. It is **not** an immutable software or institutional specification. Civizen should be able to improve beyond individual structures proposed in the book. Future editions of the book may incorporate learning produced through Civizen implementation, research, pilots, institutional experience, governance, and contributors. **Book → informs Civizen → Civizen tests and learns → models improve → future book editions may incorporate learning.** This does not weaken Civizen's overall purpose.

Achieving that purpose requires more than a technology platform.

Civizen needs an institutional architecture capable of:

- protecting its mission over generations;
- preventing capture by any founder, corporation, government, political movement, investor, donor, or other interest group;
- allowing meaningful participation by individuals and institutions;
- receiving and responsibly deploying public, philanthropic, institutional, and potentially private investment;
- developing and operating shared technological infrastructure;
- protecting essential intellectual property while maintaining appropriate openness;
- establishing legitimate and auditable governance;
- enabling collaboration across jurisdictions;
- developing and operating pilot programs;
- supporting long-term international expansion;
- creating mechanisms through which contributors can progressively assume responsibility for the system;
- and ensuring institutional continuity beyond any individual leader.

This Blueprint defines the institutional structure through which those objectives can be achieved.

It is not intended to establish a new world government or replace national and local institutions.

Civizen is designed as a **shared institutional and technological framework** through which humanity can progressively develop common standards, interoperable systems, shared infrastructure, and mechanisms for cooperation while preserving cultural diversity, languages, communities, and appropriate national and local governance.

The living philosophy is [`philosophy-of-mature-humanity.md`](../00-foundation/philosophy-of-mature-humanity.md). The public mission charter is [`the-civizen-charter.md`](../00-foundation/the-civizen-charter.md). The controlling long-term citizenship pathway is [`recognized-planetary-citizenship-pathway.md`](../00-foundation/recognized-planetary-citizenship-pathway.md). Present legal status is described in [`current-legal-status-notice.md`](../02-policies/institutional/current-legal-status-notice.md).

## 2. Institutional Design Principles

The institutional architecture of Civizen should be governed by the following principles.

### 2.1 Mission Before Ownership

No individual or organization should be able to own Civizen in the conventional sense.

Legal entities may hold assets, intellectual property, contracts, funds, and operational responsibilities, but these assets should ultimately be held and managed in service of Civizen's mission.

### 2.2 No Permanent Founder Control

The founder may provide initial leadership, direction, intellectual foundations, and institutional continuity during the formative stages of Civizen.

However, the institutional architecture must deliberately transition from founder-led development toward durable institutional governance.

The founder should remain an important guardian of the project's original purpose, but should not possess permanent unilateral authority over Civizen.

### 2.3 Protection Against Capture

The system must be designed so that control cannot be acquired merely through:

- capital;
- political power;
- nationality;
- organizational size;
- technical control;
- number of users;
- donations;
- institutional status;
- or temporary popularity.

Major institutional safeguards should require distributed approval.

### 2.4 Separation of Functions

Civizen should not attempt to place every activity inside one organization.

Different institutional functions require different governance, financing, legal, and operational structures.

At minimum, the architecture should distinguish between:

1. **Mission Stewardship**
2. **Technology and Infrastructure**
3. **Participatory Governance**
4. **Programs and Pilots**
5. **Research and Standards**
6. **Commercial Activities**
7. **Funding and Investment**
8. **Institutional Partnerships**

### 2.5 Legal Governance and Participatory Governance Are Different

Civizen's legal entities must have legally accountable governing bodies.

The wider Civizen community should simultaneously develop participatory governance mechanisms through which contributors, experts, communities, institutions, and ultimately citizens can shape the system.

These two systems should interact, but they should not be confused.

A community vote cannot automatically eliminate the fiduciary responsibilities imposed on directors of a legal entity.

Likewise, a legal board should not become the permanent owner of Civizen's social and governance system.

### 2.6 Transparency by Default

Institutional decisions, funding relationships, conflicts of interest, governance rules, major contracts, system changes, and allocation principles should be transparent whenever legally and practically possible.

### 2.7 Progressive Decentralization

Civizen should not attempt artificial decentralization before functioning institutions exist.

Its development should move through deliberate stages:

**Founder-led formation → Institutional governance → Distributed participation → Mature participatory governance**

Decentralization should increase as the system's capacity, participation, security, and institutional maturity increase.

### 2.8 Global Participation, Local Implementation

Civizen should establish principles and systems capable of global interoperability while allowing implementation to reflect legitimate local circumstances.

The objective is not uniformity.

The objective is compatibility around essential shared standards.

## 3. Proposed Institutional Architecture

Civizen should initially be built around a **mission-first institutional structure**, rather than creating multiple corporations before their functions are necessary.

The proposed long-term architecture contains several layers.

These layers are **proposed**. They have not yet been legally established.

### 3.1 Civizen Foundation

#### Primary role

The Civizen Foundation should become the principal steward of Civizen's public-interest mission.

Its responsibilities may include:

- protecting the Civizen mission;
- maintaining constitutional and institutional documents;
- stewarding the Civizen name, identity, domains, and core mission-related intellectual property;
- administering grants and philanthropic funding;
- coordinating public-interest programs;
- commissioning research;
- developing shared standards;
- supporting pilot programs;
- maintaining institutional partnerships;
- facilitating participatory governance;
- protecting the project from capture;
- and ensuring continuity across generations.

#### Initial legal direction

The current working recommendation is to evaluate formation as a **California nonprofit public benefit corporation**, followed by an application for federal tax-exempt status under IRC §501(c)(3), assuming the final purposes and activities are structured to satisfy the relevant requirements.

California provides a specific nonprofit public benefit corporation form, while federal 501(c)(3) qualification requires an organization to be organized and operated for qualifying exempt purposes.

The final legal structure should be established with qualified nonprofit and tax counsel before formation.

#### Transitional option

Before the Foundation obtains its own operating and tax infrastructure, an appropriate **fiscal sponsorship arrangement** could potentially allow selected charitable programs to receive grant or philanthropic support through an established nonprofit.

Fiscal sponsorship should be treated as a transitional mechanism rather than Civizen's permanent institutional identity.

Entity-planning notes (not formation documents) are in [`28-entity-legal-and-implementation-action-plan-v0.1.md`](../04-operations/funding-and-budget/28-entity-legal-and-implementation-action-plan-v0.1.md).

## 4. Civizen Technologies

Civizen should **not automatically create a commercial corporation simply because it operates technology**.

The need for a separate commercial entity should arise from actual operational requirements.

Potential triggers include:

- raising equity investment;
- commercial contracts incompatible with Foundation operations;
- revenue-generating technology services;
- commercial licensing;
- substantial enterprise services;
- employment or compensation structures better suited to a commercial entity;
- acquisition of commercial businesses;
- or other activities that should remain legally and financially separate from the charitable institution.

When those triggers arise, a separate mission-locked commercial organization may be appropriate.

The working model is:

**Civizen Technologies, Public Benefit Corporation**

A Delaware Public Benefit Corporation is one possible form to evaluate. Delaware law expressly provides for public benefit corporations under its General Corporation Law.

This commercial entity is **proposed**. It has not been formed by this Blueprint.

The Foundation and the commercial company should not simply become parent and subsidiary by default.

Their relationship must be deliberately designed to prevent either:

- commercialization of the public-interest mission, or
- inappropriate transfer of charitable assets for private benefit.

The exact ownership, licensing, board appointment, governance, and economic relationship should therefore be determined with legal and tax counsel when the commercial entity becomes necessary.

A commercial operating company, if later formed, would not own Civizen's civic mission, citizen identity, or participatory governance system.

## 5. Institutional Structure at Maturity

The mature Civizen ecosystem can be understood as five interacting institutional layers.

### Layer 1: Mission Stewardship

**Civizen Foundation**

Protects purpose, constitutional principles, institutional continuity, and public-interest assets.

### Layer 2: Participatory Governance

**Civizen Governance System**

Provides mechanisms through which individuals, contributors, experts, communities, and institutions participate in decisions.

This is not itself synonymous with the legal board.

### Layer 3: Knowledge and Standards

**Councils, Institutes, Working Groups and Expert Networks**

Develop evidence, standards, methodologies, policies, proposals, and recommendations across Civizen's areas of activity.

### Layer 4: Programs and Implementation

**Civizen Programs, Pilots and Institutional Partnerships**

Universities, governments, NGOs, international organizations, communities, companies, researchers, and other partners can collaborate on specific implementations without having to assume control of Civizen itself.

### Layer 5: Economic and Technological Operations

**Civizen Technologies and other approved operating entities**

Build and operate commercial or scalable infrastructure where a separate economic structure is required.

## 6. Governance Architecture

Civizen requires several forms of governance rather than a single governing body.

How authority is distributed and exercised among those institutions is defined in the subordinate [`governance-framework.md`](./governance-framework.md) (Working Governance Framework). Do not merge that Framework into this Blueprint.

Current public governance policy is [`governance-and-human-oversight.md`](../02-policies/institutional/governance-and-human-oversight.md). Community participation rules are in the [`civizen-community-governance-charter.md`](../02-policies/governance/civizen-community-governance-charter.md). Governance models and drafts are indexed in [`01-governance/README.md`](../01-governance/README.md).

### 6.1 Fiduciary Board

The Civizen Foundation should initially have an independent Board of Directors with genuine fiduciary responsibility.

A provisional structure is:

**Formation stage:** approximately 5 directors  
**Expansion stage:** approximately 7 to 9 directors

The board should progressively include expertise in areas such as:

- institutional governance;
- law;
- economics and finance;
- technology and AI;
- education;
- health;
- international development;
- public policy;
- ethics;
- culture;
- environmental systems;
- and civil society.

No single stakeholder category should dominate the board.

This board is **proposed**. No such Foundation board currently exists as a formed legal organ of Civizen.

### 6.2 Founder Role

During the formation stage, the founder should provide:

- institutional vision;
- continuity;
- interpretation of the Mature Humanity framework;
- development leadership;
- external representation;
- and stewardship of the transition from concept to functioning institution.

The founder role should gradually evolve from **primary decision-maker** toward **founding steward and institutional guardian**.

The Blueprint should eventually specify:

- founder powers during each stage;
- limits on those powers;
- reserved matters;
- succession procedures;
- emergency succession;
- removal protections and mechanisms;
- and the point at which founder-specific authority expires.

Those details are elaborated in the [Founder Transition & Succession Framework](./founder-transition-succession-framework.md). That Framework is a specialized implementation of this section and of §15; it must not be merged into this Blueprint.

### 6.3 Councils

Civizen may establish specialized councils rather than attempting to place every type of expertise on the fiduciary board.

Possible councils include:

- Scientific and Research Council
- Ethics and Governance Council
- Technology and AI Council
- Education Council
- Health Council
- Culture Council
- Environment Council
- Economic Systems Council
- Institutional Partners Council
- Citizen and Contributor Council

These should generally advise, research, evaluate, or propose rather than automatically exercise corporate authority.

Related draft: [`advisory-high-council-model-v0.1.md`](../01-governance/institutions/advisory-high-council-model-v0.1.md).

### 6.4 Participatory Governance

Civizen's digital governance infrastructure should progressively enable:

- proposal submission;
- structured deliberation;
- expert review;
- evidence presentation;
- amendments;
- voting;
- delegation;
- public reasoning;
- implementation tracking;
- and retrospective evaluation.

Participation rights do not necessarily need to be identical for every category of decision.

Different decisions may legitimately involve different combinations of:

- citizens;
- contributors;
- affected communities;
- subject-matter experts;
- institutional participants;
- elected or selected representatives;
- fiduciary directors;
- and independent reviewers.

### 6.5 Role of Artificial Intelligence

AI should remain an **advisory and analytical participant**, not the sovereign decision-maker.

AI may assist with:

- research;
- analysis;
- simulation;
- detecting inconsistencies;
- evaluating evidence;
- summarizing deliberation;
- modeling consequences;
- identifying risks;
- suggesting alternatives;
- detecting possible manipulation;
- and making recommendations.

Final legitimate authority must remain attributable to human and institutional governance mechanisms.

Related public policy: [`ai-advisory-and-human-authority.md`](../02-policies/institutional/ai-advisory-and-human-authority.md).

## 7. Constitutional and Reserved Matters

Certain decisions should receive substantially greater protection than ordinary operational decisions.

These may include:

- changing Civizen's fundamental mission;
- transferring core Civizen intellectual property;
- selling or permanently licensing essential system assets;
- dissolving the Foundation;
- merging Civizen into another institution;
- abandoning open governance commitments;
- changing fundamental non-discrimination principles;
- altering essential protections against institutional capture;
- converting core public-interest infrastructure into exclusively private property;
- or transferring ultimate control to a government, corporation, investor, nation, political organization, or individual.

Such **Reserved Matters** may eventually require approval across multiple governance bodies rather than a simple board majority.

For example:

**Board approval + independent mission review + participatory approval**

The exact mechanism should be developed later in the Governance Constitution.

The current community charter is [`civizen-community-governance-charter.md`](../02-policies/governance/civizen-community-governance-charter.md). An earlier constitution draft is retained as historical: [`civizen-constitution-v0.1.md`](../01-governance/constitution/civizen-constitution-v0.1.md). A future Civizen Constitution / Governance Constitution, when written, should elaborate this Blueprint rather than replace its mission-lock principles.

## 8. Intellectual Property Stewardship

Civizen's intellectual property should be divided according to function rather than treated as one undifferentiated asset.

Possible categories include:

### A. Foundational Intellectual Property

Includes the *Mature Humanity* book and independently created foundational works.

Existing creator rights can remain intact while Civizen receives sufficiently durable rights to use relevant material for its institutional mission.

### B. Civizen Institutional Assets

Potentially including:

- Civizen trademarks;
- domains;
- constitutional documents;
- institutional standards;
- governance protocols;
- identity assets;
- and other mission-critical materials.

These should ultimately receive strong mission-lock protections.

### C. Open-Source Technology

Software intended to constitute common Civizen infrastructure should use clearly defined open-source licensing appropriate to the project's objectives.

### D. Commercial Technology

Certain enterprise services, infrastructure, integrations, or commercially developed components may require different licensing arrangements.

The boundary between open infrastructure and commercial services should be explicit and publicly understandable.

Related public policy: [`open-source-ip-brand-stewardship.md`](../02-policies/institutional/open-source-ip-brand-stewardship.md).

## 9. Funding Architecture

Civizen should be capable of receiving multiple forms of capital without allowing any single form of capital to determine its mission.

Potential funding channels include:

- philanthropic donations;
- grants;
- government grants and contracts;
- international development funding;
- university and research funding;
- institutional partnerships;
- foundation funding;
- program sponsorship;
- public-interest research funding;
- earned revenue;
- technology and service revenue;
- impact-oriented investment;
- and, where appropriate, commercial equity investment through a separate commercial entity.

Funding rights and governance rights should remain distinct.

**Providing more money should not automatically provide greater authority over Civizen's mission.**

The detailed economics for:

- investors;
- contributors;
- institutional partners;
- employees;
- contractors;
- community participants;
- program beneficiaries;
- and future revenue-sharing mechanisms

should therefore be governed by separate economic frameworks consistent with the institutional architecture.

Those frameworks are the future **Funding Constitution** and **Contributor Framework**. Specific percentages and distribution formulas should remain in those instruments rather than being permanently fixed in this Institutional Blueprint.

### 9.1 Non-commercial development and previously contemplated investor allocation

Civizen's previously contemplated investor/proceeds allocation should not automatically remain allocated to investors when development and funding have instead been provided substantially or entirely through governments, nonprofit institutions, grants, public-interest programs, universities, international organizations, or other non-commercial sources.

If Civizen becomes fully or substantially funded and developed through such non-commercial support, particularly where this support enables Civizen to become operational, self-sustaining, and capable of generating revenue, some or all of the economic allocation that otherwise could have been reserved for private investors may instead become available for distribution among qualifying Civizen contributors and institutional partners.

This may include individuals who make significant contributions through institutional partnerships even when those contributions fall outside their normal funded, salaried, contracted, or formally paid scope.

However:

- no contributor should automatically obtain a financial entitlement simply by participating;
- paid work should not normally be counted again as an uncompensated contribution unless there is clearly identifiable additional contribution beyond the paid scope;
- institutional funding, individual contribution, paid professional work, volunteer contribution, intellectual contribution, technological contribution, relationship-building, implementation work, and other forms of contribution must be distinguishable;
- contribution should be recorded continuously;
- the size, importance, quality, duration, uniqueness, responsibility, and measurable impact of contributions should be documented;
- contribution records should support future recognition, compensation, proceeds allocation, governance eligibility, or other benefits where appropriate;
- economic allocation should ultimately follow transparent rules rather than discretionary founder decisions;
- specific percentages and distribution formulas should remain governed by the Funding Constitution and Contributor Framework rather than being permanently fixed in this Institutional Blueprint.

Non-commercial funding and partnership do not confer government control, investor ownership, or automatic private claims on Civizen.

### 9.2 Capital, work, institution, and authority

**Capital contribution ≠ work contribution ≠ institutional contribution ≠ governance authority.**

All can be recognized, but they should not automatically produce identical rights.

- **Capital contribution** may be recognized through lawful funding instruments where those instruments exist.
- **Work contribution** may be recognized through employment, contracts, volunteer records, attribution, or later allocation rules.
- **Institutional contribution** may be recognized through partnership, research, implementation, or funding-partner roles.
- **Governance authority** arises only through valid legal, fiduciary, or participatory mechanisms — not automatically from money, work, or institutional status.

### 9.3 Related funding instruments

A future **Funding Constitution** should govern detailed allocation, eligibility, and proceeds rules. Until that constitution is adopted:

- current public funding integrity policy is [`funding-and-financial-integrity.md`](../02-policies/institutional/funding-and-financial-integrity.md);
- investor inquiries are governed by [`investor-interest-non-offering-notice.md`](../02-policies/institutional/investor-interest-non-offering-notice.md);
- strategic (non-selected-model) guidance is [`funding-and-sustainability-plan.md`](../01-governance/funding-and-monetary/funding-and-sustainability-plan.md);
- an earlier financing constitution is retained as historical / superseded: [`funding-constitution-v0.1.md`](../01-governance/funding-and-monetary/funding-constitution-v0.1.md);
- planning models live under [`04-operations/funding-and-budget/`](../04-operations/funding-and-budget/README.md).

Historical or scenario percentage pools are **not** current public policy and are not adopted by this Blueprint.

## 10. Institutional Participation

Civizen should not view organizations merely as donors, customers, or sponsors.

Organizations can participate in different institutional capacities.

These may include:

- **Founding Institutional Partners**
- **Research Partners**
- **Academic Partners**
- **Government Partners**
- **International Organization Partners**
- **Implementation Partners**
- **Technology Partners**
- **Funding Partners**
- **Community Partners**
- **Standards Partners**
- **Pilot Partners**
- **Knowledge Partners**
- **Service Providers**

An organization may belong to several categories simultaneously.

Its role should be determined by its actual capabilities, areas of expertise, resources, responsibilities, and intended contribution rather than merely by its institutional type.

This principle should guide the [Stakeholder & Partnership Framework](./stakeholder-partnership-framework.md), which consolidates the Stakeholder Map, partnership strategy, partnership roles, and modular partnership packages. The Stakeholder Map is the future living operational dataset derived from that Framework.

Existing related public policy: [`international-partnerships-and-chapters.md`](../02-policies/institutional/international-partnerships-and-chapters.md). Institutional identity: [`institutional-identity-and-relationship.md`](../02-policies/institutional/institutional-identity-and-relationship.md).

Partnership does not transfer ownership of Civizen or create authority over the initiative unless such authority is expressly established through valid governing documents and applicable law.

## 11. Pilot Architecture

Civizen should not define Phase 1 around a single universal pilot.

Different institutional partners have different:

- expertise;
- infrastructure;
- populations;
- jurisdictions;
- funding capacity;
- research capability;
- implementation capacity;
- and strategic interests.

Civizen should therefore maintain a **portfolio of pilot models**.

A partnership may begin with:

- one pilot;
- several related pilots;
- or a broader multi-domain implementation.

Whenever possible, pilots should involve complementary partners from different sectors rather than relying entirely on one institution.

For example, a pilot might combine:

**University + government body + civil-society organization + technology partner + participating community**

The Stakeholder Map should therefore precede detailed pilot matchmaking.

Related existing documents: the [**Pilot Framework**](./pilot-framework.md) is the parent institutional reference. Operational children include [`community-readiness-and-program-availability-framework.md`](../01-governance/participation/community-readiness-and-program-availability-framework.md), [`country-activation-framework-v0.1.md`](../01-governance/country-activation/country-activation-framework-v0.1.md), and [`14-pre-major-build-validation-program-v0.1.md`](../04-operations/funding-and-budget/14-pre-major-build-validation-program-v0.1.md).

## 12. Relationship With Governments and International Institutions

Governments are important Civizen stakeholders, but no national government should own or control Civizen.

Governments may participate as:

- funders;
- regulators;
- implementation partners;
- data partners;
- research partners;
- standards participants;
- pilot hosts;
- service partners;

International organizations may play similar roles at regional or global levels.

Civizen should remain institutionally capable of working with competing or politically different governments without becoming an instrument of any of them.

## 13. Citizens and Contributors

The ultimate legitimacy of Civizen cannot come exclusively from institutional partnerships.

Civizen exists for people.

The institutional model must therefore create progressively stronger channels through which individuals can participate as:

- citizens;
- contributors;
- researchers;
- professionals;
- volunteers;
- developers;
- educators;
- reviewers;
- voters;
- delegates;
- community organizers;
- institutional representatives;
- and eventually stewards.

Contribution should lead to responsibility and meaningful participation, not merely symbolic membership.

Participation, citizenship, scoring, volunteering, or partnership does **not** automatically create an economic claim, equity interest, revenue right, or investor-equivalent entitlement.

### 13.1 Contribution, compensation, and non-commercial funding

The same allocation principle stated in Funding Architecture applies to people who build, advise, research, implement, or otherwise contribute to Civizen.

If development and funding are provided substantially or entirely through governments, nonprofit institutions, grants, public-interest programs, universities, international organizations, or other non-commercial sources — particularly where that support enables Civizen to become operational, self-sustaining, and capable of generating revenue — some or all of the economic allocation that otherwise could have been reserved for private investors may instead become available for distribution among qualifying Civizen contributors and institutional partners.

This may include individuals who make significant contributions through institutional partnerships even when those contributions fall outside their normal funded, salaried, contracted, or formally paid scope.

However:

- no contributor should automatically obtain a financial entitlement simply by participating;
- paid work should not normally be counted again as an uncompensated contribution unless there is clearly identifiable additional contribution beyond the paid scope;
- institutional funding, individual contribution, paid professional work, volunteer contribution, intellectual contribution, technological contribution, relationship-building, implementation work, and other forms of contribution must be distinguishable;
- contribution should be recorded continuously;
- the size, importance, quality, duration, uniqueness, responsibility, and measurable impact of contributions should be documented;
- contribution records should support future recognition, compensation, proceeds allocation, governance eligibility, or other benefits where appropriate;
- economic allocation should ultimately follow transparent rules rather than discretionary founder decisions;
- specific percentages and distribution formulas should remain governed by the **Funding Constitution** and **Contributor Framework** rather than being permanently fixed in this Institutional Blueprint.

**Capital contribution ≠ work contribution ≠ institutional contribution ≠ governance authority.** All can be recognized, but they should not automatically produce identical rights.

### 13.2 Contribution Record

Civizen must preserve a persistent **Contribution Record** (or an equivalent institutional mechanism) sufficient to make later recognition and compensation possible.

The Civizen system should eventually be capable of maintaining auditable records of meaningful contributions by:

- individuals;
- organizations;
- researchers;
- developers;
- professionals;
- volunteers;
- institutional representatives;
- pilot participants;
- implementation partners;
- and other contributors.

The Contribution Record should distinguish kinds of contribution rather than collapsing them into a single undifferentiated score.

Detailed Contribution Record design is in the [Contributor Framework](./contributor-framework.md). This Blueprint retains the institutional principles: who contributed; in what capacity; duration; responsibility; quality, uniqueness, and impact; paid, unpaid, or mixed; and whether additional contribution exceeded a funded, salaried, contracted, or formally paid scope.

This Blueprint does **not** specify the technical design of the record. An automated score alone must not determine compensation. Existing in-app contribution, score, or recognition features are not themselves an allocation engine and do not create proceeds claims.

The Contribution Record should support — where later rules so provide — recognition, compensation, proceeds allocation, governance eligibility, or other benefits. It does not, by itself, confer those benefits.

### 13.3 Related contributor instruments

The [**Contributor Framework**](./contributor-framework.md) elaborates eligibility, recording, review, and any economic participation *evidence* rules. It is working institutional design, not adopted public policy.

Until economic participation rules are separately adopted:

- current public contributor policy is [`contributor-participation-and-recognition.md`](../02-policies/institutional/contributor-participation-and-recognition.md) (`/contribute/policy`);
- compensation **planning** (not the Contributor Framework) is [`21-contributor-compensation-and-in-kind-framework-v0.1.md`](../04-operations/funding-and-budget/21-contributor-compensation-and-in-kind-framework-v0.1.md);

## 14. Institutional Development Stages

The institution should develop progressively.

### Stage 0: Founder-Led Formation

Current stage.

Primary objectives:

- articulate the philosophy;
- build the initial Civizen system;
- document the architecture;
- establish public identity;
- develop institutional credibility;
- establish preliminary partnerships;
- and define the initial legal structure.

### Stage 1: Institutional Formation

Establish the mission-holding institution.

Key objectives:

- legal formation;
- initial independent board;
- governance policies;
- IP arrangements;
- financial controls;
- fundraising infrastructure;
- institutional partnership agreements;
- and initial pilot programs.

### Stage 2: Multi-Institutional Expansion

Civizen becomes a collaborative institutional platform.

Key objectives:

- multiple pilots;
- university participation;
- governmental collaboration;
- international partnerships;
- working councils;
- research programs;
- growing contributor network;
- and formalized participatory governance.

### Stage 3: Distributed Governance

Authority progressively moves from the founding institution toward structured participatory governance.

The Foundation increasingly becomes a guardian of mission, constitutional integrity, shared assets, and system continuity.

### Stage 4: Mature Civizen System

Civizen functions as durable shared infrastructure whose legitimacy, knowledge, operations, governance, and resources are distributed across a broad global institutional and citizen network.

No individual institution is indispensable to its continuation.

## 15. Institutional Continuity and Succession

Civizen must be designed from the beginning to survive:

- its founder;
- individual directors;
- individual funders;
- individual governments;
- individual technology providers;
- individual institutional partners;
- and major political or economic changes.

Institutional continuity mechanisms should therefore include:

- succession planning;
- independent governance;
- documented constitutional principles;
- distributed institutional knowledge;
- mission-locked assets;
- transparent financial controls;
- open technical infrastructure where appropriate;
- redundant operational capacity;
- conflict-of-interest rules;
- board renewal procedures;
- emergency governance procedures;
- and protection against hostile institutional capture.

The ultimate test of Civizen's institutional maturity will be whether the system can remain faithful to its purpose without depending on the continued authority of any one person.

## 17. Institutional Objective

Civizen should eventually become neither a conventional company nor simply another nonprofit organization.

Its long-term objective is to become a **durable civic institution and shared infrastructure for human cooperation**.

The legal organizations created around Civizen are instruments for achieving that purpose.

They are not the purpose themselves.

The institutional architecture must therefore continuously answer one fundamental question:

> **Does this structure help humanity participate together, make better collective decisions, protect shared interests, and progressively build a more peaceful, fair, capable, and mature civilization?**

If an institutional structure ceases to serve that purpose, Civizen should be able to improve the structure without abandoning the principles it exists to protect.
