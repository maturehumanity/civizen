# Civizen Stakeholder & Partnership Framework

**Status: Working Stakeholder & Partnership Framework**

**Working Draft 0.1**

This document is a **working stakeholder and partnership framework** and an authoritative project reference. It describes proposed partnership roles, modules, evaluation processes, and a future Stakeholder Map. It does **not** assert that named partnership titles, modules, agreements, Founding Institutional Partner designations, or the Stakeholder Map already exist as operational programs.

Proposed partnership mechanisms remain **proposed** until they are established through valid agreements, governing documents, and applicable law.

## Institutional hierarchy

This Framework is **subordinate to** the [Civizen Institutional Blueprint](./institutional-blueprint.md) and must operate consistently with the [Civizen Governance Framework](./governance-framework.md). The institutional family must not be merged.

| Document | Role |
| --- | --- |
| [**Institutional Blueprint**](./institutional-blueprint.md) | Defines Civizen's institutional architecture. |
| [**Governance Framework**](./governance-framework.md) | Defines how authority is distributed. |

Those names should not become competing parent documents. The **Stakeholder Map** is the future living operational dataset derived from this Framework.

Public operational partnership rules remain in [`international-partnerships-and-chapters.md`](../02-policies/institutional/international-partnerships-and-chapters.md) until expressly updated. That public notice is narrower than this Framework and stays controlling for `/partners` copy.

## 1. Purpose

The Civizen Stakeholder & Partnership Framework defines:

- who Civizen's principal stakeholders are;
- how potential partners should be identified and evaluated;
- the different roles organizations and individuals may play;
- what Civizen may ask of partners;
- what Civizen may offer partners;
- how partnerships should be structured;
- how contributions should be recorded;
- how conflicts and institutional influence should be managed;
- and how partnerships should lead to concrete pilots, programs, research, infrastructure, or other measurable outcomes.

This Framework sits beneath the:

- [**Civizen Institutional Blueprint**](./institutional-blueprint.md), which defines the institutional architecture;
- and the [**Civizen Governance Framework**](./governance-framework.md), which defines how authority is distributed.

It should be read together with the [**Pilot Framework**](./pilot-framework.md), which defines what Civizen tests with those participants.

This Framework does not grant governance control to partners.

Participation, funding, institutional status, political authority, expertise, implementation capacity, and contribution may each produce legitimate rights or responsibilities, but they should not automatically produce identical authority.

---

## 2. Partnership Philosophy

Civizen should not approach institutions merely as:

- donors;
- sponsors;
- customers;
- vendors;
- or endorsers.

The objective is to develop a network of institutions and individuals capable of collectively building, testing, improving, implementing, financing, studying, and sustaining Civizen.

A partnership should therefore answer:

> **What can this stakeholder meaningfully contribute to Civizen, what can Civizen meaningfully provide in return, and what shared outcome can we produce together?**

Partnerships should be outcome-oriented rather than symbolic.

## 3. Stakeholders Are Multi-Dimensional

Civizen should not classify organizations solely by institutional type.

For example, a university may contribute:

- education;
- healthcare;
- research;
- AI;
- public policy;
- economics;
- engineering;
- culture;
- environmental work;
- governance research;
- entrepreneurship;
- or implementation infrastructure.

Likewise, a government may contribute:

- funding;
- data;
- legal authorization;
- public infrastructure;
- service delivery;
- population access;
- research;
- regulation;
- pilots;
- procurement;
- or implementation.

A stakeholder therefore should be understood through several dimensions simultaneously.

These include:

1. **Institutional Type**
2. **Field or Domain**
3. **Geographic Scope**
4. **Capabilities**
5. **Resources**
6. **Potential Contribution**
7. **Strategic Relevance**
8. **Implementation Capacity**
9. **Funding Capacity**
10. **Influence and Network Reach**
11. **Research Capacity**
12. **Population or Community Access**
13. **Alignment with Civizen Principles**
14. **Risk**
15. **Potential Partnership Role**

## 6. Partnership Roles

An organization should be able to assume one or several partnership roles.

Potential roles include:

### Strategic Partner

Works with Civizen across multiple programs or domains.

### Founding Institutional Partner

Provides significant support during Civizen's formative institutional stage.

This title should be used selectively and should not imply ownership.

### Pilot Partner

Participates directly in designing or implementing a Civizen pilot.

### Implementation Partner

Provides operational capacity necessary to execute a program.

### Research Partner

Conducts or supports research relevant to Civizen.

### Academic Partner

Provides faculty, students, educational programs, research, facilities, or academic validation.

### Government Partner

Provides lawful public-sector collaboration.

### International Partner

Supports cross-border or multinational cooperation.

### Technology Partner

Provides technical systems, infrastructure, development, or expertise.

### Funding Partner

Provides grants, public funding, institutional funding, program funding, donations, or other non-equity financial support.

### Investment Partner

Provides investment capital through an appropriate commercial structure.

Investment partnership should not imply mission governance.

### Knowledge Partner

Provides specialized expertise, information, research, methodologies, or professional knowledge.

### Standards Partner

Participates in developing or validating shared standards.

### Community Partner

Connects Civizen with local populations or communities.

### Evaluation Partner

Provides independent assessment of programs and outcomes.

### Service Partner

Provides professional or operational services.

### Infrastructure Partner

Provides physical, digital, financial, organizational, or communications infrastructure.

### Scaling Partner

Helps move a validated Civizen system or program from pilot scale toward wider implementation.

## 7. Partnership Is Not a Single Tier System

Civizen should avoid treating partnerships as a simple prestige hierarchy such as:

Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum.

The importance of a partnership depends on its actual role.

A small research group may contribute knowledge essential to Civizen's success.

A large institution may provide substantial funding but little intellectual contribution.

A municipality may provide a population-scale implementation environment.

A university may supply multidisciplinary expertise.

A technology company may provide infrastructure.

These contributions are fundamentally different and should be recorded as such.

- and measurable social impact.

## 11. Contribution Recording

Partnerships should produce a persistent record of meaningful contributions.

This implements, for partnerships, the **Contribution Record** designed in the [Contributor Framework](./contributor-framework.md) (principle in the [Institutional Blueprint](./institutional-blueprint.md) §13.2). It does not create a separate contribution-scoring algorithm. Participation does not automatically create a financial entitlement.

The Record should capture both **institutions** and **individual participants working through those institutions**.

Partnership-specific records should distinguish:

**contracted work**  
**institutionally funded work**  
**voluntary contribution**  
**donated resources**  
**investment**  
**grant funding**  
**extraordinary contribution beyond paid scope**

Those distinctions are essential for later recognition and potential compensation. Generic Record fields (contributor, type, dates, evidence, validation, duration, outcomes, impact, attribution) are specified in the Contributor Framework.

---

## 12. Individual Contributions Within Institutional Partnerships

Institutional partnerships should not erase the contribution of the people actually doing the work.

For example, an employee, researcher, faculty member, public servant, developer, or other professional may contribute substantially beyond the work their institution formally funded or required.

Where possible, Civizen should separately record:

- the institution's contribution;
- the individual's contribution;
- the individual's paid responsibilities;
- and additional meaningful contribution beyond that paid scope.

Such recording does not automatically create a financial entitlement.

It preserves evidence for later consideration under the Contributor Framework, Funding Constitution, compensation systems, recognition systems, or other adopted mechanisms.

---

## 13. Partnership Contributions and Economic Participation

The economic treatment of partnerships should follow Civizen's broader principle:

**Funding source and actual contribution matter.**

Where Civizen is substantially or fully developed through:

- government support;
- nonprofit funding;
- university collaboration;
- philanthropic grants;
- international institutions;
- donated infrastructure;
- or other non-commercial assistance,

economic allocations that otherwise might have been required to compensate private investors may potentially become available for qualifying contributors or partners.

The exact allocation must remain governed by:

- the future **Funding Constitution** (not this Framework; percentages are not fixed here);
- the [**Contributor Framework**](./contributor-framework.md) (evidence and recognition design, not a compensation formula);
- contractual arrangements;
- legal requirements;
- and adopted compensation rules.

Until those instruments are adopted, current public integrity policy is [`funding-and-financial-integrity.md`](../02-policies/institutional/funding-and-financial-integrity.md). An earlier financing constitution is historical: [`funding-constitution-v0.1.md`](../01-governance/funding-and-monetary/funding-constitution-v0.1.md). Contributor recognition policy is [`contributor-participation-and-recognition.md`](../02-policies/institutional/contributor-participation-and-recognition.md).

Participation alone does not create an economic claim.

## 14. Partnership and Governance

Partnership does not automatically create Civizen-wide governance authority.

Partners may appropriately exercise authority over:

- their own commitments;
- jointly funded programs;
- pilot implementation;
- contractual matters;
- professional areas within their legitimate expertise;
- and decisions affecting their responsibilities.

Partners should not acquire general constitutional authority merely because they:

- provide funding;
- represent a government;
- have a large workforce;
- have high institutional prestige;
- provide critical technology;
- host a pilot;
- or contribute infrastructure.

Governance participation should follow the [Civizen Governance Framework](./governance-framework.md).

**Capital contribution ≠ work contribution ≠ institutional contribution ≠ expertise ≠ governance authority.** Funding, institutional prestige, government status, technical dependency, population size, or major contribution must not automatically create unilateral Civizen control. Investment must not create mission ownership. Government partnership must not create government ownership.

---

## 15. Partnership Independence and Capture Prevention

Civizen should avoid becoming dependent on any one partner to the extent that the partner can effectively control the system.

Risks may include excessive dependency on:

- one funder;
- one government;
- one technology provider;
- one cloud provider;
- one university;
- one political jurisdiction;
- one philanthropic institution;
- one implementation organization;
- or one major commercial partner.

Where practical, Civizen should develop:

- diversified partnerships;
- alternative suppliers;
- portable infrastructure;
- distributed institutional relationships;
- clear IP arrangements;
- transparent funding;
- and exit mechanisms.

---

## 16. Partner Evaluation

Potential partners should be assessed against several factors.

These may include:

### Mission Alignment

Does the organization substantially align with Civizen's public-interest purpose?

### Relevant Capability

Can it contribute something Civizen actually needs?

### Quality

Does the institution have demonstrated competence?

### Integrity

Does it operate in a way compatible with Civizen's ethical standards?

### Implementation Capacity

Can it execute, not merely discuss?

### Strategic Value

Can the relationship materially advance Civizen?

### Reach

Does it provide access to communities, sectors, expertise, infrastructure, or networks?

### Funding Capacity

Can it provide or help secure resources?

### Research Capacity

Can it create or validate useful knowledge?

### Scaling Capacity

Can it help move successful pilots toward broader implementation?

### Independence Risk

Would Civizen become dangerously dependent on the institution?

### Reputation Risk

Could the relationship materially compromise Civizen's credibility or mission?

### Governance Risk

Could the institution attempt to convert funding or participation into control?

### Conflict Risk

Does the institution have material interests that conflict with the program?

---

## 17. Partnership Qualification

Potential partnerships may be categorized provisionally as:

### Exploratory

Initial conversations are taking place.

### Candidate

There is reasonable strategic fit and potential for a defined collaboration.

### Active

A formal or operational partnership exists.

### Strategic

The partner is involved across multiple areas or plays an important long-term role.

### Completed

The specific partnership or pilot has concluded.

### Suspended

The relationship is temporarily halted.

### Closed

The relationship has ended.

These statuses should describe relationship state rather than institutional prestige.

A normal partnership pathway may include:

1. Stakeholder identification
2. Capability mapping
3. Preliminary fit assessment
4. Initial engagement
5. Mutual-interest discovery
6. Opportunity definition
7. Pilot or program matching
8. Scope definition

10. Contribution definition

14. Implementation
15. Contribution recording
16. Monitoring
17. Evaluation
18. Expansion, renewal, or completion

## 19. Pilot-Oriented Partnership Development

Civizen should avoid approaching prospective partners with only a broad request such as:

> Fund Civizen.

Where possible, Civizen should instead identify specific collaboration opportunities.

For example:

> Civizen is developing an Education pilot that requires an academic research partner, implementation institution, participating population, technology support, and independent evaluation.

This allows institutions to see exactly where they can participate.

It also enables Civizen to build **multi-stakeholder pilot coalitions** rather than relying on one organization to provide everything.

---

## 20. Multi-Stakeholder Pilot Coalitions

A Civizen pilot should generally seek complementary capabilities.

A possible coalition may include:

**Lead Implementation Partner**  
+ **Academic / Research Partner**  
+ **Government or Public Institution**  
+ **Community or Population Partner**  
+ **Technology Partner**  
+ **Funding Partner**  
+ **Independent Evaluation Partner**

The exact combination should depend on the pilot.

One organization may perform several roles.

Not every pilot requires all roles.

---

## 21. Pilot Matching

Partners should be matched to pilots based on:

- fields of expertise;
- geographic relevance;
- available populations;
- implementation capacity;
- institutional mandate;
- funding;
- research capability;
- technology;
- infrastructure;
- and strategic interest.

Civizen should therefore not define a universal pilot independently from partners.

It should maintain a portfolio of possible pilots and configure them with appropriate partners.

---

## 22. Partnership Packages

Rather than rigid commercial packages, Civizen should develop **modular partnership packages**.

A partner may select or combine relevant modules.

Potential modules include:

### Research Module

Partner contributes research, methodology, faculty, researchers, or evaluation.

### Pilot Module

Partner participates directly in implementation.

### Technology Module

Partner contributes software, AI, infrastructure, cybersecurity, identity, or other technology.

### Funding Module

Partner provides grants, institutional funding, program funding, or other financial resources.

### Community Module

Partner provides access to or representation of participating communities.

### Public Infrastructure Module

Government or public institution provides facilities, systems, authorization, or public-service integration.

### Knowledge Module

Partner contributes professional expertise.

### Standards Module

Partner helps develop or validate common standards.

### Education and Training Module

Partner contributes educational content, training, certification, instructors, or learning environments.

### Evaluation Module

Partner independently measures outcomes.

### Scaling Module

Partner helps replicate or expand validated systems.

A major institution may participate through multiple modules simultaneously.

## 23. Founding Institutional Partners

During Civizen's formative stage, a limited number of organizations may qualify as **Founding Institutional Partners**.

The designation should recognize substantial early contribution rather than provide ownership.

Potential criteria may include:

- significant early funding;
- essential research;
- major implementation;
- substantial infrastructure;
- strategic institutional credibility;
- major technology contribution;
- multi-domain participation;
- or exceptional support during Civizen's formative stage.

Founding Institutional Partner status should not create:

- permanent governance control;
- ownership of Civizen;
- permanent veto power;
- or automatic economic rights outside adopted agreements.

## 24. Partnership Agreements

Formal partnerships should define, where relevant:

- purpose;
- scope;
- responsibilities;
- deliverables;
- contribution;
- funding;
- intellectual property;
- data rights;
- privacy;
- attribution;
- governance boundaries;
- conflicts;
- duration;
- evaluation;

Agreements should be proportionate to the relationship.

A research collaboration does not require the same agreement as a national-scale implementation.

## 25. Intellectual Property

Partnerships should clearly determine ownership or licensing of outputs.

Possible categories include:

- pre-existing partner IP;
- pre-existing Civizen IP;
- jointly created IP;
- open-source outputs;
- public-interest research;
- commercial technology;
- data;
- standards;
- and educational materials.

Partners should not unintentionally acquire ownership of Civizen's core institutional identity or constitutional assets merely through project participation.

---

## 26. Data and Privacy

Partnerships involving data must define:

- what data is collected;
- who controls it;
- who can access it;
- why it is needed;
- how long it is retained;
- whether it can be reused;
- security responsibilities;
- participant rights;
- and applicable legal requirements.

Civizen should avoid partnerships whose value depends on exploitative use of participant data.

---

## 27. Transparency

Material partnership relationships should be disclosed where appropriate.

Transparency may include:

- partner identity;
- role;
- funding;
- program involvement;
- material conflicts;
- major contributions;
- and outcomes.

Legitimate confidentiality may remain necessary for:

- security;
- protected data;
- negotiations;
- personal privacy;
- proprietary technology;
- or legal requirements.

---

## 28. Partnership Review

Active partnerships should be periodically reviewed.

Review may evaluate:

- performance;
- contribution;
- outcomes;
- mission alignment;
- ethics;
- dependency;
- conflicts;
- public impact;
- participant experience;
- and whether continuation remains beneficial.

A partnership should not continue merely because it was historically important.

---

## 29. Ending Partnerships

Civizen should retain the ability to end relationships that:

- compromise the mission;
- create unacceptable conflicts;
- fail repeatedly;
- misuse Civizen's identity;
- violate agreements;
- create unacceptable legal or ethical risk;
- attempt institutional capture;
- or substantially harm participants.

Termination should follow appropriate contractual and governance procedures.

---

## 30. Stakeholder Map

Civizen should maintain a living Stakeholder Map derived from this Framework.

The Stakeholder Map should not merely be a directory of organizations.

For every candidate stakeholder, it should eventually record fields such as:

- organization;
- institutional type;
- domains;
- geography;
- capabilities;
- potential roles;
- relevant programs;
- resources;
- funding capacity;
- contacts;
- strategic relevance;
- relationship status;
- contribution history;
- risks;
- potential pilot match;
- and next action.

This map should become an operational instrument for partnership development.

The Stakeholder Map is **not yet implemented** as an application database, CRM, or public directory of candidate partners. This Framework establishes the requirement and field set. Building that dataset or UI is future work and is not part of this documentation update.

---

## 31. Stakeholder Prioritization

Civizen should prioritize institutions based on **fit with immediate objectives**, not prestige alone.

Early partners should ideally help Civizen obtain some combination of:

- validation;
- implementation;
- evidence;
- users or participating communities;
- expertise;
- institutional credibility;
- technology;
- funding;
- and scaling capacity.

The strongest early partnership may therefore be a coalition of complementary organizations rather than one globally famous institution.

---

## 32. Initial Partnership Strategy

During the current formative stage, Civizen should prioritize building a small number of credible, complementary partnerships capable of producing real pilots.

The early objective should not be:

> Acquire as many partners as possible.

It should be:

> Build the smallest credible coalition capable of demonstrating that a Civizen system can work in practice.

This coalition should ideally combine multiple sectors.

---

## 33. Relationship to Funding Strategy

Partnership development and fundraising should operate together.

Some institutions may primarily contribute:

- money.

Others may contribute:

- populations;
- expertise;
- technology;
- facilities;
- legitimacy;
- research;
- implementation;
- or regulatory support.

Civizen should therefore evaluate the total value of partnerships rather than comparing them solely by cash funding.

In-kind and professional contribution should be recorded separately from financial funding.

Related funding strategy (no financing model selected): [`funding-and-sustainability-plan.md`](../01-governance/funding-and-monetary/funding-and-sustainability-plan.md). Inquiry-readiness: [`04-operations/funding-and-budget/README.md`](../04-operations/funding-and-budget/README.md).

## 34. Relationship to Governance

This Framework does not define partner governance rights.

Those rights are governed by the [Civizen Governance Framework](./governance-framework.md).

Partners may participate in governance when appropriate to:

- their expertise;
- implementation responsibility;
- affected interests;
- institutional mandate;
- or defined governance role.

Institutional prestige or financial contribution alone should not create system-wide voting authority.

## 36. Relationship to the Pilot Framework

This Framework answers:

> **Who should Civizen work with and how should the partnership work?**

The Pilot Framework should answer:

> **What should Civizen test with those partners, how should the pilot be designed, and how should success be measured?**

## 37. Partnership Objective

The long-term objective is not to build a network of institutions around Civizen as passive supporters.

It is to enable governments, universities, companies, nonprofits, international organizations, professionals, communities, researchers, and individuals to become active participants in building systems that serve their shared world.

A successful Civizen partnership should create something that none of the participating parties could have achieved as effectively alone.
