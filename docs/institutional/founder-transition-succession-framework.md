# Civizen Founder Transition & Succession Framework

**Status: Working Founder Transition & Succession Framework**

**Working Draft 0.1**

This document is a **working founder transition and succession framework** and an authoritative project reference. It describes proposed transition stages, founder roles, succession procedures, and official-use activation. It does **not** assert that those stages, roles, procedures, or activation workflows already exist as adopted law or operational product features.

Proposed transition mechanisms remain **proposed** until they are established through valid institutional formation, governing documents, and applicable law. This Framework is institutional design, not executed bylaws, articles, employment terms, contracts, board resolutions, trusts, or IP assignments.

This Framework is a **specialized implementation** of principles already contained in the [Civizen Institutional Blueprint](./institutional-blueprint.md) (§§6.2 and 15) and the [Civizen Governance Framework](./governance-framework.md) (§7). It must not be merged into those documents.

## Current conceptual stage

Treat Civizen's present condition as **F0 — Founder-Led Development** unless other project documentation clearly establishes a later stage.

The application may be publicly accessible (website, accounts, Testing/Live channels) while remaining experimental, developmental, prototype, or unfinished. **Public access does not by itself trigger founder transition.**

**Founder transition is based on functional self-sufficiency, not percentage of development and not official-use designation alone.** Official operational use increases the need for strong institutional controls, but founder access should not be removed from a domain or component merely because that component becomes publicly or officially used.

A component should transition away from founder-only supervision when it has sufficient mechanisms to operate safely and accountably without that dependency.

The preferred conceptual transition is:

**Founder supervision → institutional capability built → parallel/verified operation → demonstrated self-sufficiency → founder steps back from that function**

Transition can occur part by part. During formative design, founder authority may include substantial model revision; as a component becomes self-sufficient, model evolution should increasingly follow institutional governance. This Framework does not authorize reducing founder permissions.

## 1. Purpose

The Civizen Founder Transition & Succession Framework defines how the founder's authority, responsibilities, access, stewardship, and succession should evolve as Civizen progresses from an early-stage project into a functioning public institution.

The Framework exists to balance two legitimate requirements:

1. Civizen must have sufficiently strong founding leadership while it is still being designed and developed.
2. Civizen must not permanently depend on or become controlled by any single individual, including its founder.

The objective is therefore not to remove the founder prematurely.

It is to create a deliberate transition from:

**Founder-led creation → Institutional stewardship → Distributed governance → Long-term continuity**

The transition should occur according to Civizen's actual maturity and public responsibilities rather than merely according to time.

---

## 2. Public Availability Is Not Official Public Deployment

Civizen distinguishes between:

### Public Availability

The application, website, source materials, prototypes, documentation, demonstrations, or experimental features may be publicly accessible.

People may:

- explore Civizen;
- create accounts;
- test features;
- provide feedback;
- contribute;
- participate in experiments;
- or help develop the system.

Public availability by itself does not mean Civizen has become an officially deployed public civic institution.

During this stage, substantial portions of Civizen may remain:

- experimental;
- incomplete;
- under development;
- subject to major redesign;
- unavailable to most users;
- or explicitly presented as prototypes or pilots.

### Official Public Deployment

Official public deployment occurs when Civizen deliberately designates one or more systems as operational for real public or institutional reliance.

This may occur:

- system-wide;
- by domain;
- by jurisdiction;
- by program;
- by service;
- or by governance function.

Official deployment creates materially greater obligations concerning:

- reliability;
- governance;
- accountability;
- security;
- access;
- continuity;
- due process;
- institutional responsibility;
- and protection against unilateral control.

Founder authority should therefore transition primarily in response to **official operational maturity**, not simply because a publicly accessible website exists.

The current publicly accessible website, accounts, and Testing/Live app channels are **public availability**, not official public deployment of Civizen as a civic institution. See [`ENVIRONMENT_LIFECYCLE.md`](../04-operations/dev/ENVIRONMENT_LIFECYCLE.md).

## 3. Founder Role During Formation

During the formative stage, the founder may exercise broad operational and developmental authority.

This may reasonably include:

- product direction;
- project architecture;
- documentation;
- development priorities;
- feature design;
- experimental governance design;
- early data models;
- branding;
- institutional strategy;
- partner development;
- fundraising strategy;
- initial team selection;
- development access;
- system administration;
- test-data administration;
- prototype modification;
- and rapid correction of unfinished systems.

This authority is justified by the project's formative condition.

It should not be interpreted as permanent ownership of Civizen governance.

---

## 4. Founder Development Access

While Civizen remains primarily under development, founder access may include broad or full administrative capability where technically necessary.

This may include access to:

- unfinished functionality;
- internal development interfaces;
- configuration;
- governance prototypes;
- test environments;
- experimental workflows;
- internal documentation;
- administrative controls;
- debugging;
- data migration;
- feature flags;
- and other development facilities.

The existence of broad founder access during development does not itself violate Civizen's distributed-governance principles.

The relevant question is:

> **Does this domain or component have sufficient mechanisms to operate safely and accountably without founder-only supervision?**

Official operational use increases the need for strong institutional controls. It does **not** automatically remove founder access.

---

## 5. Development Authority vs Governance Authority

Civizen should distinguish between:

**Development authority**

and:

**institutional governance authority.**

During early development, the founder may need authority to restructure software, documentation, prototypes, interfaces, schemas, and workflows rapidly.

That does not necessarily mean the founder should ultimately have unilateral authority over:

- adopted constitutional principles;
- public rights;
- official elections;
- participant sanctions;
- institutional assets;
- public funding allocations;
- legally established bodies;
- adopted public standards;
- or irreversible high-impact decisions.

This distinction should become increasingly explicit as Civizen matures.

---

## 6. Transition Should Be Capability-Based

Founder authority should not automatically disappear:

- after a fixed number of years;
- after a funding round;
- after a particular number of users;
- when the source code becomes public;
- simply when the website is publicly accessible;
- because a percentage of development is complete;
- or merely because a component is designated for official operational use.

Transition should depend on **functional self-sufficiency**: whether the component can operate safely and accountably without founder-only supervision.

Official operational use increases the need for strong institutional controls, but founder access should not be removed from a domain or component merely because that component becomes publicly or officially used.

The preferred conceptual transition is:

**Founder supervision → institutional capability built → parallel/verified operation → demonstrated self-sufficiency → founder steps back from that function**

Transition can occur part by part.

During formative design, founder authority may include substantial **model revision** (taxonomy, Score, contribution measurement, and similar). As a component becomes self-sufficient, model evolution for that component should increasingly follow institutional governance. See [Shared Classification & Model Evolution Architecture](../03-platform/model-evolution/shared-classification-and-model-evolution-architecture.md). This Framework does **not** change current founder permissions.

Relevant capabilities may include:

- reliable identity verification;
- roles;
- authorization;
- moderation;
- governance;
- auditing;
- safety controls;
- technical administration;
- recovery;
- continuity;
- institutional responsibility;
- qualified alternative stewards;
- an established mission-holding organization;
- an independent fiduciary board;
- documented policies;
- appropriate appeals and review;
- emergency processes;
- succession procedures;
- and other mechanisms required by that component.

---

## 7. Founder Transition Stages

### F0 — Founder-Led Development

Civizen remains primarily a developing project.

Characteristics may include:

- highly incomplete system;
- experimental features;
- prototype governance;
- founder-directed architecture;
- broad founder administrative access;
- limited institutional structure;
- early partnerships;
- test or sample data;
- and rapid product changes.

Founder authority is broad.

This is the current conceptual stage.

F0–F5 are a **conceptual** sequence. They are not an application workflow and are not adopted as bylaws. Broad founder bootstrap access in the current product is consistent with F0. Do not reduce that access from this documentation update.

---

### F1 — Structured Development

Civizen has additional contributors, partners, and working groups.

Responsibilities begin to be delegated.

Characteristics may include:

- defined contributor roles;
- domain assignments;
- partner involvement;
- pilot teams;
- clearer permissions;
- internal review;
- documented development processes;
- and growing institutional oversight.

The founder remains the primary system steward but no longer performs or decides everything directly.

---

### F2 — Institutional Formation

A mission-holding organization and formal institutional governance begin operating.

Characteristics may include:

- fiduciary board;
- formal financial controls;
- legal policies;
- institutional agreements;
- executive responsibilities;
- conflicts procedures;
- documented governance;
- and formal program oversight.

Founder authority becomes institutionally bounded.

---

### F3 — Official Limited Deployment

One or more Civizen systems are formally designated for real operational use.

Examples could include:

- an officially operating pilot;
- a partner institution relying on Civizen;
- a production contribution system;
- operational service delivery;
- an adopted institutional workflow;
- or another formally deployed system.

At this stage:

- production permissions should become more structured;
- founder actions affecting live users should be auditable;
- major decisions should follow established governance;
- direct production intervention should increasingly be exceptional rather than routine.

Official limited deployment **increases** the need for those controls. It is **not** an automatic founder-access removal event. Founder access for a component should recede only when that component has demonstrated functional self-sufficiency (§6).

---

### F4 — Broad Public / Institutional Deployment

Civizen operates substantial systems relied upon by broader populations or institutional networks.

Founder authority should now primarily exist through formally defined roles.

Operational administration, governance, auditing, security, finance, and appeals should be institutionally distributed.

The founder should no longer function as an unrestricted super-administrator merely by virtue of being the founder.

---

### F5 — Mature Institutional Stewardship

Civizen can continue operating reliably without founder operational involvement.

The founder's continuing role may include:

- Founding Steward;
- institutional guardian;
- board or council participation;
- philosophy and mission stewardship;
- advisory participation;
- public representation;
- long-term institutional development;
- or another appropriately defined role.

The founder remains an important participant but is no longer the operational single point of authority.

---

## 8. Domain-by-Domain Transition

Civizen does not need to transition all authority simultaneously.

Different areas may mature at different times.

For example:

- Education may reach institutional deployment before Health.
- Contribution systems may mature before public governance.
- Civic voting may remain experimental while another service is operational.
- One jurisdiction may run an official pilot while the global system remains developmental.

Therefore transition may occur:

- by domain;
- feature;
- jurisdiction;
- program;
- pilot;
- or institutional function.

This enables Civizen to mature progressively rather than pretending that the entire system becomes "finished" at one moment.

Do not introduce one global `Civizen is live` switch unless an existing architecture already requires it. Testing vs Live is a **release channel**, not official-use activation of civic systems.

---

## 9. Founder Role in Official Systems

Once a Civizen component is officially deployed, founder involvement should follow the same principles of accountable authority that apply to others.

The founder may retain substantial formal responsibility.

However, official systems should increasingly distinguish between:

- normal operating authority;
- development access;
- emergency access;
- governance authority;
- and constitutional safeguards.

The founder should not use development-level access to bypass adopted governance processes.

---

## 10. Founder Emergency Access

There may be circumstances in which the founder or other highly authorized technical stewards must intervene rapidly.

Examples include:

- serious security vulnerability;
- data corruption;
- infrastructure failure;
- active fraud;
- system compromise;
- critical deployment failure;
- or immediate participant harm.

Emergency authority should eventually be:

- logged;
- limited;
- reviewable;
- proportionate;
- temporary;
- and subject to retrospective oversight.

Emergency access should not become an alternative governance channel.

The current application has a **staff emergency-access / impersonation** workflow (`governance_emergency_access_requests` and related audit events), used to assume another user's session with reason, approval, TTL, and logging. That is not the same as this Framework's founder emergency production-authority concept, and it does not replace institutional succession. See [`governance-implementation-roadmap-v0.1.md`](../04-operations/dev/governance-implementation-roadmap-v0.1.md).

---

## 11. Founder Mission Stewardship

The founder has a legitimate role in protecting the original purpose of Civizen, particularly during early institutional development.

This may include identifying situations in which proposals appear inconsistent with foundational principles.

However, mission stewardship should progressively become institutional rather than personally discretionary.

Long-term mission protection should be supported through:

- constitutional principles;
- reserved matters;
- distributed approvals;
- independent review;
- institutional records;
- and mission-locked structures.

Civizen's principles should eventually be protected because the **system protects them**, not merely because the founder remains able to intervene.

---

## 12. Founder Safeguards During Transition

Premature removal of founder authority can be as dangerous as permanent founder control.

During transition, appropriate founder protections may therefore include:

- defined board role;
- protection from arbitrary removal during clearly specified formation stages;
- participation in constitutional review;
- mission-stewardship rights;
- access to institutional information;
- ability to raise formal objections;
- and participation in succession design.

Such safeguards should be documented and limited rather than implied.

---

## 13. Founder Reserved Objection

During appropriate formation stages, the founder may have the right to issue a formal **Founder Mission Objection** when a proposed action appears to fundamentally contradict Civizen's founding mission.

Such an objection should not necessarily constitute a permanent personal veto.

Instead, it may trigger:

- reconsideration;
- independent review;
- constitutional review;
- enhanced approval requirements;
- or broader deliberation.

This protects mission integrity without making one individual permanently sovereign.

The **Founder Mission Objection** is conceptual. It is not implemented in governance code and is not a permanent unilateral veto.

---

## 14. No Permanent Founder Ownership of Governance

The founder should not possess permanent unilateral authority to:

- dissolve Civizen for personal reasons;
- privately transfer institutional control;
- appropriate mission assets;
- permanently override legitimate governance;
- sell constitutional control;
- assign governance authority to heirs;
- convert Civizen into a privately controlled system;
- or prevent lawful institutional succession indefinitely.

Founder status itself should not become inheritable governance ownership.

---

## 15. Founder Economic Rights and Governance Rights

Economic rights and governance rights should remain distinguishable.

If the founder has legitimate rights relating to:

- pre-existing intellectual property;
- compensation;
- commercial interests;
- proceeds;
- reimbursement;
- or other economic arrangements,

those rights should be governed by appropriate agreements.

They should not automatically create permanent political or institutional control.

Likewise, reducing founder governance authority should not automatically eliminate legitimate contractual or economic rights.

This Framework does **not** alter or reinterpret existing founder economic rights, IP rights, proceeds, investment arrangements, or compensation. Economic rights and governance rights are not identical. Public economic copy remains in [`funding-and-financial-integrity.md`](../02-policies/institutional/funding-and-financial-integrity.md) and related notices.

---

## 16. Intellectual Property

Founder-created intellectual property that predates institutional formation may remain subject to the founder's legitimate ownership rights unless transferred or licensed.

Civizen should establish sufficiently durable institutional rights to mission-critical assets so that the system cannot become inoperable because of succession, incapacity, dispute, or death.

Relevant arrangements may include:

- assignment;
- licensing;
- stewardship arrangements;
- open licensing;
- or other legally appropriate mechanisms.

The precise structure should be determined with appropriate professional legal advice when formalized.

Do not transfer, relicense, or alter intellectual property from this Framework. Public stance: [`open-source-ip-brand-stewardship.md`](../02-policies/institutional/open-source-ip-brand-stewardship.md). Mission-critical operational assets (repository, deployment, domains, databases, signing, stores) currently appear structurally dependent on founder/operator stewardship; detailed ownership is kept in the access-controlled operations store, not in this public document.

---

## 17. Succession

Civizen must be able to continue if the founder:

- voluntarily steps back;
- becomes unavailable;
- becomes incapacitated;
- dies;
- is unable to perform the role;
- or chooses another long-term role.

Succession should therefore be designed before it becomes necessary.

No operational founder-succession procedure, multi-person credential escrow, or institutional continuity package is adopted in this repository. Related design principles live in the [Institutional Blueprint](./institutional-blueprint.md) §15 and the [Governance Framework](./governance-framework.md) §7. Funding due diligence currently lists key-person / succession as a gap ([`19-funding-due-diligence-index-v0.1.md`](../04-operations/funding-and-budget/19-funding-due-diligence-index-v0.1.md)).

---

## 18. Succession Is Institutional, Not Personal Inheritance

The founder role should not automatically pass:

- to a family member;
- to an heir;
- to a business associate;
- or to any personally designated individual

as if Civizen were inheritable property.

The founder may participate substantially in identifying and preparing potential successors.

Final institutional authority should follow established governance procedures.

---

## 19. Types of Succession

Succession may concern several distinct responsibilities.

These should not necessarily transfer to one person.

Possible succession areas include:

### Executive Leadership

Day-to-day institutional leadership.

### Technical Stewardship

Responsibility for core technology and infrastructure.

### Mission Stewardship

Protection and interpretation of Civizen's foundational purpose.

### Governance Stewardship

Maintenance of governance procedures and institutional integrity.

### Public Representation

External communication and institutional relationships.

### Intellectual Stewardship

Historical and conceptual understanding of Civizen and Mature Humanity.

Separating these functions reduces dependence on finding one individual who must replace the founder in every respect.

---

## 20. Planned Succession

A planned transition may include:

1. identifying responsibilities;
2. documenting institutional knowledge;
3. distributing access;
4. training successors;
5. delegating authority;
6. establishing oversight;
7. testing continuity;
8. formally transferring defined responsibilities;
9. preserving founder advisory participation where appropriate.

---

## 21. Emergency Succession

Civizen should also prepare for unexpected founder unavailability.

Emergency succession procedures should eventually define:

- who temporarily assumes necessary responsibilities;
- who obtains critical access;
- how credentials are recovered;
- how institutional assets remain accessible;
- how urgent decisions are authorized;
- how the board or governing body is convened;
- and how a permanent arrangement is subsequently established.

No essential Civizen system should depend permanently on credentials known only to one person.

---

## 22. Technical Continuity

As Civizen approaches official deployment, critical infrastructure should progressively avoid single-person dependencies.

This includes:

- source repositories;
- domains;
- hosting;
- production credentials;
- backups;
- certificates;
- financial accounts;
- deployment infrastructure;
- databases;
- signing keys;
- app-store accounts;
- security systems;
- institutional documentation;
- and other critical resources.

Appropriate access should be distributed securely and auditable.

Public documentation describes **that** production access exists and is restricted; it does **not** publish HOW-to, credentials, or host topology. See [`AGENTS.md`](../04-operations/dev/AGENTS.md) (Production operations) and [`ENVIRONMENT_LIFECYCLE.md`](../04-operations/dev/ENVIRONMENT_LIFECYCLE.md). This Framework does not change ownership or credentials.

---

## 23. Institutional Knowledge

Founder knowledge should progressively be converted into institutional knowledge.

Important information should be preserved through:

- documentation;
- architectural records;
- governance records;
- decision histories;
- operating procedures;
- rationale documentation;
- partner histories;
- legal records;
- technical documentation;
- and institutional memory.

Civizen should not depend upon undocumented knowledge held by one person.

---

## 24. Successor Qualification

Individuals assuming significant stewardship roles should be evaluated according to factors such as:

- integrity;
- competence;
- institutional understanding;
- commitment to Civizen's mission;
- independence;
- relevant experience;
- ability to collaborate;
- judgment;
- public-interest orientation;
- and absence of disqualifying conflicts.

Popularity, wealth, founder proximity, or organizational prestige alone should not determine succession.

---

## 25. Removal and Accountability

No stewardship role should become immune from accountability.

The founder and future stewards should ultimately operate under appropriately defined processes for:

- review;
- conflict-of-interest management;
- suspension;
- removal;
- appeal;
- and succession.

The exact legal mechanics may depend on the institutional form and adopted governing documents.

---

## 26. Relationship to User Roles and Permissions

Founder transition should eventually be reflected in the product's authorization architecture.

During development, the founder may retain broad bootstrap access.

As components become officially deployed, permissions should increasingly follow explicit roles such as:

- development administrator;
- production administrator;
- governance steward;
- institutional officer;
- auditor;
- security administrator;
- council role;
- or other defined responsibilities.

The founder should eventually receive access because of formally assigned roles, not merely because the account is permanently hard-coded as `founder`.

**Current product (F0, unchanged by this documentation task):** the `founder` app role is a bootstrap authority with full `APP_PERMISSIONS` (same class as `admin` and `system`), including a client-side fallback so a flaky permissions RPC cannot lock founders out of admin routes. `FOUNDER_BASE_PERMISSIONS` is a narrower list that is **not** what the live `founder` role currently receives. Production vs development is distinguished as Testing vs Live **release channels** (`updates.test`), not as separate founder permission sets. Do not modify `APP_PERMISSIONS`, RBAC, founder bootstrap, or schemas from this Framework.

---

## 27. Official-Use Activation

A Civizen component should have a clear mechanism by which it moves from:

**Experimental / Developmental**

to:

**Officially Operational**

Activation criteria may include:

- technical readiness;
- security review;
- governance readiness;
- institutional responsibility;
- legal review;
- support capability;
- monitoring;
- participant safeguards;
- data protections;
- continuity;
- and authorized approval.

The activation process should be appropriate to the risk of the system.

Official-use activation is **not implemented**. Related existing mechanisms that may later support it include: Testing vs Live ([`ENVIRONMENT_LIFECYCLE.md`](../04-operations/dev/ENVIRONMENT_LIFECYCLE.md)); `updates.test`; budget `draft → under_review → approved` with separate `publish_flag`; civic-election sample vs live rows; document status vocabulary; community/program readiness; feature registry / feature flags. None of these currently means a component is officially operational for civic reliance.

Official-use designation is not, by itself, a founder-access removal event. Founder access recedes when the component is functionally self-sufficient (§6).

---

## 28. Official Deployment Record

When a Civizen component becomes officially operational, Civizen should preserve a record identifying:

- what was activated;
- date;
- scope;
- jurisdiction;
- responsible institution;
- responsible administrators;
- applicable governance;
- participant rights;
- applicable policies;
- technical version;
- and authorization.

This makes it clear which parts of Civizen remain experimental and which parts people may officially rely upon.

---

## 29. Reversion

Official deployment need not be irreversible.

A system may return to:

- limited deployment;
- pilot status;
- maintenance;
- suspension;
- or redevelopment

if significant problems emerge.

Such reversion should follow appropriate governance and participant-protection procedures.

---

## 30. Founder Participation After Transition

Transition should not be interpreted as requiring the founder to disappear from Civizen.

The founder may continue indefinitely as a highly active contributor, leader, thinker, steward, representative, council participant, or other legitimate role.

The essential distinction is:

> **Continued influence through contribution, trust, responsibility, and defined institutional roles is compatible with Civizen. Permanent unilateral control is not.**

---

## 31. Mature Founder Role

At institutional maturity, an appropriate founder role may include:

**Founder & Founding Steward**

Potential responsibilities may include:

- mission continuity;
- historical interpretation;
- strategic advice;
- institutional representation;
- constitutional consultation;
- long-term vision;
- mentorship;
- and participation in designated governance bodies.

The final title and authority should depend on Civizen's actual institutional development.

---

## 32. Relationship to Governance Framework

The Governance Framework defines how authority is distributed across Civizen.

This Framework explains how founder-specific authority moves into that broader structure.

Where the two overlap, founder authority should progressively become subject to the same governance principles applicable to comparable responsibilities.

See [Governance Framework](./governance-framework.md) §7.

---

## 33. Relationship to Institutional Blueprint

The Institutional Blueprint establishes the principle of progressive institutionalization and continuity beyond the founder.

This Framework operationalizes that principle.

See [Institutional Blueprint](./institutional-blueprint.md) §§6.2 and 15.

---

## 34. Relationship to Pilot Framework

Pilots may operate while founder authority remains substantial.

However, official pilot responsibilities should still be documented.

As pilots move toward operational adoption, responsibility should increasingly move from informal founder control toward explicit institutional roles.

See the [Pilot Framework](./pilot-framework.md). Official-use activation of a pilot is not the same as public availability of a prototype.

---

## 35. Relationship to Technology Governance

The future Technology Governance Framework should define detailed controls for:

- production access;
- administrative privileges;
- privileged credentials;
- deployment;
- emergency technical intervention;
- security;
- audit logs;
- and infrastructure stewardship.

This Founder Transition Framework establishes only the institutional principle.

Related architecture: [`decentralized-transition-architecture.md`](../03-platform/decentralization/decentralized-transition-architecture.md) and [`03-platform/README.md`](../03-platform/README.md). A standalone Technology Governance Framework remains planned.

## 36. Founder Transition Objective

The goal is neither to preserve founder control indefinitely nor to remove it before Civizen can safely operate without it.

The correct transition occurs when Civizen can replace personal control with functioning institutional capacity.

The founder's greatest long-term success would therefore not be to remain indispensable.

It would be to help create a Civizen system capable of faithfully continuing its purpose without requiring any single individual to control it.
