# Governance and Human Oversight

## Governance Layers

Civizen distinguishes among four forms of governance.

### 1. Legal and Fiduciary Governance

The governing body of any legal entity operating Mature Humanity or Civizen is responsible for duties imposed by applicable law, including oversight of assets, officers, conflicts, finances, risk, compliance, and mission.

Community votes, software rules, tokens, scores, advisory councils, and AI systems cannot displace those legal duties.

### 2. Institutional and Program Governance

Authorized directors, officers, employees, committees, and institutional partners administer programs, budgets, policies, partnerships, and operations within their delegated authority.

### 3. Community Governance

Participants may submit proposals, discuss policies, provide evidence, vote in advisory or delegated processes, select representatives where authorized, and review published decisions.

Community governance applies only within the scope expressly assigned to it.

### 4. Technical and Protocol Governance

Maintainers and participating contributors may propose, review, test, approve, or reject software and protocol changes under published technical procedures.

Open-source development does not transfer legal ownership of trademarks, domains, confidential information, or organizational assets.

## Current Bootstrap Stage

Civizen is currently founder-led.

During the bootstrap stage, the founder and authorized administrators may perform functions that later become distributed among directors, officers, maintainers, committees, independent reviewers, and community representatives.

Bootstrap authority is operational and transitional. It does not establish unlimited or permanent personal control.

## Human Authority

Only authorized human decision-makers may:

- exercise fiduciary authority,
- approve binding contracts,
- approve budgets or payments,
- accept restricted funds,
- impose material disciplinary measures,
- determine employment or compensation,
- issue official institutional positions,
- approve investment terms,
- or authorize emergency powers.

## AI Role

AI may assist with research, translation, analysis, simulation, anomaly detection, drafting, accessibility, documentation, and recommendations.

AI has no independent vote, legal office, fiduciary status, ownership right, bank authority, signing authority, spending authority, disciplinary authority, or power to make final institutional decisions.

The controlling principle is:

> AI advises. Humans decide and remain accountable.

## Current Authority Boundary and Future Development

At the current stage, Civizen governance does not by itself create a state, government, court, nationality, public office, territorial jurisdiction, or legally binding public law. Current community and technical decisions are binding only within authority lawfully delegated to Civizen.

Terms such as citizen, constitution, council, law, treasury, election, and governance describe current Civizen civic and institutional processes unless a future official instrument expressly establishes a broader lawful status.

This current boundary does not abandon Civizen's long-term mission. Civizen may help develop representative institutions, constitutional proposals, democratic processes, and international cooperation through which participating peoples and lawful institutions could later recognize planetary citizenship or broader public responsibilities. Any such authority must arise through legitimate, transparent, lawful, and accountable processes, not through terminology or software alone.

## Accountability and Appeals

Material community and institutional decisions should include:

- an identifiable decision-maker,
- a stated source of authority,
- a record of the decision,
- a reason or supporting evidence where appropriate,
- conflict disclosure,
- review or appeal procedures where appropriate,
- and correction procedures for error, abuse, or compromised authority.
